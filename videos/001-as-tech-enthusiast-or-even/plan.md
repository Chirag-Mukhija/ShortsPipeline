# Plan: AI Can Code. So What Should I Actually Learn?
Written 2026-11-12 from the creator's choices in status.md. Awaiting creator approval.

## Angle and promise
- **Angle:** Angle 3 from the brief ("AI Can Code. So What Should I Learn?"), told through the creator's own chain of switches as the spine.
- **Promise:** You'll leave knowing what's worth learning when AI writes the code and the tools keep changing, and how to use AI so you actually learn instead of just finishing things.

## For
A self-taught tech learner, early-career developer or college student in tech who learns in limited hours (after classes or after work) and keeps getting pulled sideways by the next video, tool or project.

## Length
Target ~10:00 (creator's range: 8–12 min).

## Takeaway (from research; nothing guaranteed)
"Nothing is guaranteed. But you can improve your odds: finish small real things on one main thing, learn by recalling and spacing, ask AI to explain rather than do, and park new ideas instead of chasing them."

## Structure
Each beat is something to sketch against. Sketch any beat you see differently and add it to the inbox as "for a beat" with `Beat N:` in the note.

**Beat 1: The hook (~0:00–0:30)**
- **Says:** In a few months I went from networking to a custom protocol, to Claude Code, to backend, to system design, to AWS and DevOps. Most of what I finished, an AI agent wrote. So if AI can code, what should I actually be learning?
- **On screen:** GRAPHIC: a hand-drawn chain draws itself link by link, one label per link. Each older link fades to grey as the next one appears. Cut to A-roll on the question.

**Beat 2: The chain, honestly (~0:30–2:15)**
- **Says:** Walk through the switches as they happened: each one started with a video or a new use case, and each time the last interest faded. Admit it plainly: I try to finish something substantial to feel relief after switching, but mostly what I finish is made by coding agents, not by me.
- **On screen:** A-roll, intercut with the chain graphic zooming into each link. SCREEN: the creator's real project folders (the unfinished protocol, etc.).

**Beat 3: Is switching actually bad? (~2:15–3:30)**
- **Says:** Honest answer: nobody has measured it for people like us. No study compares "one stack for a year" against "keep sampling". The closest evidence (breadth of roles predicting who becomes an entrepreneur, Stanford alumni [7]) is about founders, not employees. So switching isn't proof you're failing.
- **On screen:** GRAPHIC: a hand-drawn balance scale, "go deep" vs "try new", that never settles. A small "no study" stamp on it.

**Beat 4: The real cost isn't switching. It's not learning (~3:30–5:15)**
- **Says:** Anthropic ran a trial: 52 mostly junior engineers learned a new Python library. The AI group scored 50% on the quiz vs 67% by hand, biggest gap on debugging, and weren't meaningfully faster. Those who delegated scored under 40%; those who asked conceptual questions scored 65%+ [1]. Disclose: Anthropic makes Claude, and I use Claude Code. Limits: small sample, a sidebar assistant, quiz right after.
- **On screen:** GRAPHIC: two hand-drawn bars (50% / 67%) filling in, then a split: "delegated" vs "asked why". LOWER THIRD: "Anthropic study. I use their product."

**Beat 5: The one thing I never dropped: DSA (~5:15–6:30)**
- **Says:** The only link I've kept since the start is DSA, because I believe it'll stay relevant for at least five or six more years as the way someone's thinking and problem solving gets checked. That's my bet, not a fact.
- **On screen:** A-roll. GRAPHIC: back to the chain; every link has faded except one thick DSA thread running under all of them. SCREEN: the creator's own DSA practice (notes or solved problems).

**Beat 6: Learn skills, not tool names (~6:30–7:45)**
- **Says:** Tools on my chain changed every few weeks; thinking skills didn't. Practice helps, but it isn't magic: it explained under 1% of the differences between people in professions [4]. What does help: testing yourself and spacing your practice beat rereading and highlighting [6]. And 64% of developers now use AI to learn, but only 1% use it alone [2].
- **On screen:** GRAPHIC: chain links split into two piles, "tool names" (crossed out as they go stale) and "skills" (kept). Simple "test yourself / space it out" vs "reread / highlight" doodle.

**Beat 7: How to learn with AI so you actually learn (~7:45–9:15)**
- **Says:** Three rules: ask AI to explain, not just to do; try it again without AI before you move on; and when a shiny new idea shows up, write a short plan for it and park it instead of chasing it [5].
- **On screen:** SCREEN: a Claude Code session, the same task asked two ways ("do it" vs "explain why"). SCREEN or GRAPHIC: a parking note with one new idea and when the creator will look at it.

**Beat 8: Close (~9:15–10:00)**
- **Says:** I'm still sorting this out myself. Nothing guarantees results, but you can improve your odds: one main thing, small real finishes, recall and spacing, AI that explains, and parked ideas.
- **On screen:** GRAPHIC: the chain redrawn: one thick main link, small parked side-links hanging off it. A-roll to end.

## Look and feel
The creator's call: a hand-drawn, iPad-sketch look instead of the usual clean dark dev UI.
- **Palette:** off-white paper background, dark graphite ink lines, one accent color for "the thing that lasts" (the DSA thread, the "skills" pile), faded grey for links that fade.
- **Type:** a handwritten-style font for labels; one clean sans for numbers and sources so stats stay legible at phone size.
- **Motion:** strokes draw on, links fade out, things get crossed out. Slight wobble, no glossy transitions.
- **References:** whiteboard and sketch-note explainers; the chain metaphor carries through every beat.
- **Who draws it:** the creator wrote "this does not mean , i will do the animation myself on my ipad". Read as: the studio builds the hand-drawn look, the creator doesn't animate it. The creator can still send rough scene sketches through the inbox. Correct this at the gate if that reading is wrong.

## What the creator records
- **A-roll:** talking to camera for every beat, same spot and light throughout.
- **Screen recordings:**
  - their real project folders from the chain (protocol, backend, system design, AWS work)
  - a Claude Code session doing one small task twice: "just do it" vs "explain why, then I'll write it"
  - their own DSA practice (notes, solved problems, or a streak page)
  - a short parking note for one new idea
- **Before recording:** confirm the Beat 2 wording about finishing things with coding agents; decide whether to show any of the "what carried over" ideas (only if true for them).

## Out of scope
- Roadmaps, or which language or stack to pick
- ADHD, focus apps, productivity systems
- Salary or career-outcome claims ("generalists earn more")
- Predictions about which jobs AI will replace
- Any guarantee of results
