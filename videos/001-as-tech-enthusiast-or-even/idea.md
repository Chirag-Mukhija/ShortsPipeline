## What we know
From the idea itself (2026-11-12):
- **Topic:** why people (tech folks especially) can't stick to one thing, whether that's actually bad, and how to live with the uncertainty of "will what I master still matter in a year?" in a fast-moving AI world.
- **The creator's own story is the spine:** networking → wants to build a custom protocol → a video about a new Claude Code use case → buys Claude Code, explores backend → jumps to system design (protocol interest fades) → sees a "1 to 1 million users scalable API" video full of AWS services → leans into DevOps (system design fades). This is first-hand experience and can be used as such in the script.
- **The tension to resolve:** go deep on one or two things for a year vs. regularly try new things, given limited time (after college for students, after work hours for professionals).
- **The promised payoff:** "the one certain thing" a hard-working viewer can commit to that will definitely produce results. Research must back whatever this turns out to be; no guarantee we can't support.
- **Candidate viewers named in the idea:** students learning after college hours, working professionals learning after work, and "a normal person in any field".

## The creator's answers (2026-11-12)
- **Who it's for:** "for both a tech enthusiast self taught learner , early career developer and a college student in tech". The common thread: someone in tech who learns on their own time, with limited hours, and keeps getting pulled sideways.
- **What the viewer leaves with:** "Let research decide". Research proposes the takeaway, and it has to be backed by evidence.
